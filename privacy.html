Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 28, 2026

The short version

  • Your journal is stored on your device by default. You do not need a Tendi account.
  • Optional iCloud sync uses your own Apple account. We do not receive or read your synced journal.
  • Your iPhone's own backups include your journal. With iCloud sync on, your Apple Watch also stores a copy.
  • Analytics and on-device AI are optional and off by default.
  • We do not sell your data, share it with advertisers, or use your journal to train AI models.

Your journal on your device

Tendi stores your mood entries, notes, activities, photos, voice recordings and transcripts on your device. It also stores Important Days, settings, reminders, import and export information, insights you chose to hide, an unfinished entry as a draft, and insights calculated from your entries. Each entry records the time zone it was made in. These let the app show your journal, charts, summaries, marked dates, and reminders. Tendi also stores small working records on the device, such as check-ins from a widget, a Shortcut or your Apple Watch that are waiting to be added, and the dates of recent sync and backup activity.

Journal entries, photos, recordings, and local insight details are not sent to Tendi servers. Photos, voice notes, and imported journal data use your device's protected app storage.

Your iPhone's backups, in iCloud Backup or on a computer, include Tendi's journal with its photos and recordings, along with Tendi's settings. These backups are separate from Tendi's iCloud sync and happen even when sync is off. Apple's backup settings control them.

Journal can remember up to five recent searches, including the words and filters you used. These stay in protected storage on this device. They are not synced or included in Tendi backups or exports, and are never sent to analytics. Their storage is marked to be excluded from iPhone backups. You can remove them with Clear in Journal search or Delete All Data. If removal fails, Tendi reports it so you can try again.

When you save a note of at least 10 characters, Tendi uses Apple's on-device language tools to estimate whether the note sounds positive or negative. If that tone and the mood you chose are far apart, Tendi lets you save the entry as written or take another look. This check runs whether On-device AI is on or off. Tendi does not save the score with your entry or send it to analytics.

Widgets and Apple Watch

iPhone widgets can show your total check-in count and last logged mood or emoji. App Lock hides these summaries.

Watch-face complications can show whether you logged today and a color associated with your last mood, even when App Lock is on. That color is a visible mood signal. Complications do not show mood text, the emoji for your mood, numeric mood values, totals, notes, prompts, transcripts, photos, HealthKit details, exact locations, or other private journal content. The Quick Mood widget shows the same five mood buttons for everyone.

The Apple Watch app shows your last mood with its emoji and name, an emoji for each recent day you checked in, your total and monthly check-in counts, and your last 10 check-ins with their moods. App Lock does not apply to the Watch app. To show this, your iPhone sends the Watch a short summary, along with the activities offered there, which can include custom activity names.

When iCloud sync is on, the Watch app also stores its own synced copy of your journal on the Watch, including notes, transcripts, photos and recordings that it does not display. Turning sync off does not remove that copy. Delete All Data on your iPhone reaches it only through iCloud sync, so with sync off the copy stays on the Watch. Deleting Tendi from your Apple Watch removes it.

Lock Screen and app switcher

When App Lock is off, Important Day reminders show the day's title on the Lock Screen. With App Lock on, they say "An important day" instead. Check-in reminders use a few fixed, general phrases.

When App Lock is off, iOS can keep a picture of the last Tendi screen you saw and show it in the app switcher. With App Lock on, Tendi covers its screen when you leave the app.

Optional iCloud sync

If you enable iCloud sync, Tendi uses Apple's servers and your personal iCloud account to sync data between your Apple devices. Saved photos, voice notes, and imported journal content may sync too. We do not receive, host, or read your synced journal data.

You can turn iCloud sync off in Tendi's Settings and manage stored data through Apple's iCloud settings. A change to this setting takes effect the next time you open Tendi. If you turn sync off, syncing continues until then. Turning sync off does not remove data already in iCloud.

Restoring a Tendi backup made with Include Settings, which is on by default, can turn iCloud sync on if it was on when the backup was made. Tendi says so when the import finishes and offers Keep off.

Separately from sync, when you are signed in to iCloud, Tendi uses Apple's iCloud key-value storage to remember which "What's coming" previews you dismissed, so they stay dismissed on your other devices. It holds only those dismissals, with no journal content. This happens even when iCloud sync is off, and Delete All Data does not clear it.

Apple Health

With your permission, Tendi can read sleep, steps, and workouts for local insights. When you check in, it can also read that day's workout minutes to suggest activities. On iOS 18 and later it can write a state-of-mind sample with the mood of each check-in. Turning on Cycle Insights requests access to menstrual-flow records.

Apple Health access is optional. You can change its permissions in iOS Settings. Tendi processes HealthKit data on your device and does not send samples to Tendi servers or analytics providers. Apple Health permission and analytics consent are separate choices.

State-of-mind samples that Tendi writes stay in Apple Health when you delete an entry or use Delete All Data. You can delete them in the Health app. Apple Health may sync them through your iCloud account, depending on your Apple settings.

Restoring a Tendi backup made with Include Settings can turn Apple Health and Cycle Insights back on in Tendi without asking you again. Tendi can still read only what you have allowed in iOS.

Journaling Suggestions

When you use Apple's Journaling Suggestions picker, Tendi processes the details you select on your device. These can include prompts, media references, activities, workout summaries, place names, and photos. Only details you add or keep, such as an activity, photo, or prompt, become part of a saved entry and follow the same storage, sync, and export rules as the rest of your journal.

Optional on-device AI

On supported iPhones and languages, you can turn on AI features for related Journal search results and adaptive journaling prompts. The setting is off by default, and the AI processing runs on your iPhone.

These features may pass details from your current check-in, selected Journaling Suggestions, recent local summaries, or a saved entry to Apple's on-device language model:

  • Adaptive prompts can use your mood and energy ranges, the feeling and activities you chose, the time of day, and whether you already checked in today. They can also use a short summary of recent entries: how many there were, your recent mood trend, feelings you chose recently and activities you often log.
  • If you picked a Journaling Suggestion, prompts can also use its title, reflection prompt, suggested activities, and workout summary or step count. A title can name music or a podcast you listened to. The suggestion's place name is replaced with "a place" before the model sees it.
  • Related search themes are made from one saved entry: its mood range, activities, feeling and prompt, its note text, photo captions, and completed voice transcript.

Tendi does not send those AI inputs or outputs to Tendi servers, TelemetryDeck, or a server-side AI provider. We do not use journal content to train AI models. Journal content used by these features still follows its usual storage, iCloud sync, export, backup, and sharing rules.

AI-generated search themes are kept in a local cache, separate from your saved entries. They are excluded from iCloud sync, Tendi exports, iPhone backups, and analytics. Turning On-device AI off stops new generation and makes Journal search ignore the cached themes. It does not erase them; Delete All Data removes the cache. If Tendi cannot clear it, Tendi tells you and tries again the next time you open the app.

You choose whether to use a generated prompt. Selecting one can save it in a protected local draft, which is excluded from sync, exports, and backups. Once you save the entry, the prompt becomes part of your journal and follows the same rules as other saved details. When the model is unavailable or AI is off, Tendi uses curated prompts.

Restoring a Tendi backup made with Include Settings can turn On-device AI back on if it was on in the backup.

Optional analytics

We use TelemetryDeck to understand which parts of Tendi people use and where technical problems occur. Analytics are off by default, and you can use Tendi without enabling them. You can change your choice during onboarding or in Settings.

On iOS 26 and later, turning analytics on first asks Apple's Declared Age Range whether you are 16 or older. Analytics stay off if you are under 16, if you choose not to share your age range, or if the check cannot finish. If your device has no age range to share and Apple does not require one, you can still turn analytics on. Where Apple requires this check, onboarding asks during its privacy step, and an under-16 or declined answer there turns analytics off if they were on. Tendi learns only whether you are 16 or older, and it does not save or send that answer.

If you opt in, Tendi can send events about how you use the app:

  • which screens you open and which controls you use;
  • each step of a check-in, such as choosing a mood, feeling or activities, adding a photo, recording or editing a note, picking a prompt or a Journaling Suggestion, and saving;
  • whether a saved check-in was mood-only or had details, whether it came from the app, a widget, your Apple Watch or a Shortcut, and when entries are edited or deleted;
  • Journal search and filters, limited to which kind of filter you changed, without your search words or filter values;
  • setting changes and whether each setting is now on or off, including App Lock and whether its Face ID or passcode check succeeded, iCloud sync, On-device AI, related search, advanced mood mode, and reminders;
  • whether you allowed notifications, and which kind of notification you opened;
  • Important Days, including whether a day repeats and whether it has reminders;
  • insights, keepsakes, activities and activity groups, and the artwork you choose, identified by its collection and artist;
  • onboarding, including the page where you skipped it, and how many days you had been away when you return after a break;
  • imports and exports, each step of Delete All Data (started, confirmed, completed or failed), and technical outcomes such as failed saves or sync problems.

Some events include counts or durations in broad ranges.

Events also include random session identifiers, event times, app version and build, operating-system version, platform information, whether the app came from the App Store or TestFlight, and the analytics software version. They also include a random installation identifier, hashed before transmission, so events can be associated over time. Tendi creates a new identifier each time you turn analytics on, and when you use Delete All Data. Otherwise it stays the same. It is stored with Tendi's settings on your iPhone, so device backups include it. It is not an email address, account ID, or advertising identifier. Hashing does not make every event anonymous, and ordinary app-use events can still reveal context about how you use Tendi.

Analytics exclude journal and prompt text, mood and emotion values, activity names, search text, photos, recordings, transcripts, HealthKit samples, health measurements, cycle dates, precise locations, email addresses, and advertising identifiers. They also exclude the note tone check described above and its result. Tendi also excludes interactions with health and cycle settings, permissions and insight categories; health-history processing; wellbeing offers; breathing and grounding; and crisis-resource links. Hiding or restoring an insight sends an event without its category. Opening health or cycle context from Explore is excluded too. Opening an activity group from Explore sends the same event as viewing any other activity-group insight.

As with any internet connection, TelemetryDeck receives your IP address and the technical details iOS includes in network requests.

We do not use analytics for advertising or tracking across other companies' apps and websites, sell analytics data, or combine it with identifiable support or customer records.

Changing your analytics choice

Analytics require your explicit consent to be saved on this installation. If Tendi cannot read a valid saved choice, analytics stay off. Importing a Tendi backup keeps your current local analytics choice, including an opt-out.

Turning analytics off immediately blocks new transmissions controlled by Tendi for the current app session. Once that choice is saved, queued events cannot be sent, even after you restart Tendi or opt in again. A request already sent to the network may still complete; Tendi cannot recall it and will not retry it after you opt out.

If Tendi cannot save your choice, it shows a warning with a retry control. Analytics stay blocked for that session, but the previous saved choice may return after a restart. The warning remains if you leave onboarding or reopen Settings and clears after the choice is saved. Please retry before closing the app.

Analytics held by TelemetryDeck

TelemetryDeck says it applies additional hashing on its servers, rounds stored event times to the nearest hour, and does not store incoming IP addresses for app analytics. These practices are described in its technical description and privacy FAQ. Analytics and session identifiers can still associate events with one another.

Turning analytics off or getting a new identifier does not delete analytics already held by TelemetryDeck or request their deletion. TelemetryDeck does not guarantee a deletion deadline for cold storage and says data may remain after we close our publisher account. We cannot promise a fixed retention period or that we can identify and delete your past analytics individually.

Support emails and bug reports

When you email us or send an in-app bug report, we receive your email address and whatever you include. In-app bug reports prefill your app version, build number, device model, and operating-system version to help us troubleshoot. You can edit the message before sending it.

If Mail is not set up on your iPhone, Tendi offers to share or copy the report text instead. You choose the app it goes to, and that app handles it under its own terms.

Include sensitive journal content only if you want us to see it. For app support, write to support [at] tendijournal [dot] app. For privacy, security, or legal matters, write to hello [at] twodesks [dot] app.

Crisis support links

When you choose the Find a Helpline resource link in Tendi, it opens in Safari's in-app browser. That service may process information under its own privacy policy. Tendi does not send it your journal content, mood entries, HealthKit data, photos, voice notes, or analytics identifiers.

Sharing with other services

The services described above support optional app features, this website, email updates, payments, feedback, and support. Tendi does not send journal content to Buttondown, Buy Me a Coffee, Find a Helpline, or Fider. Anything you choose to send in a support email or post as feedback is separate from your journal in the app.

Tendi also relies on Apple services that Apple runs under its own privacy policy, such as iCloud, Apple Health, Journaling Suggestions, Declared Age Range, push notification registration, and Safari's in-app browser.

Some of the providers above are based in the United States or use servers there. Cloudflare and GitHub say they take part in the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework. Buttondown's data processing agreement uses the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses for transfers to the United States. TelemetryDeck is based in Germany, and the servers it lists are in Germany and the Netherlands.

We do not share journal content with advertising networks or data brokers.

Exporting and deleting your data

You can export your data from Tendi, delete individual entries, or use Delete All Data. Export files and Tendi backups are not encrypted, so anyone who has the file can read it. A Tendi backup also includes the insights you chose to hide, even with Include Settings off.

Delete All Data removes from this iPhone your entries with their notes, photos, recordings and transcripts, along with Important Days, custom activities and activity groups, insights you chose to hide, an unfinished entry, recent searches, the AI search-theme cache, the daily Apple Health summaries Tendi uses for insights, scheduled reminders, widget and Watch summaries, and most settings. It also gives analytics a new identifier. If Tendi cannot remove some of these, such as photos, recordings, recent searches or the AI search-theme cache, it tells you and tries again the next time you open the app.

Delete All Data does not change your iCloud sync setting or which cards you dismissed, such as "What's coming" previews. It leaves a few keychain records that hold no journal content, such as an analytics opt-out. Temporary copies of files you exported can stay in Tendi's temporary folder until iOS clears it. It also does not remove copies outside Tendi: your device backups, state-of-mind samples in Apple Health, files you exported, or a picture of the app that iOS already took for the app switcher.

With iCloud sync on, the deletions sync to iCloud and your other devices like any other change. With sync off, Delete All Data changes nothing in iCloud, and data already there can come back when you turn sync on again. Tendi records the ID of each mood entry it deletes, with no content, and removes copies that iCloud later brings back to this iPhone. Important Days, custom activities and other items have no such record, so they can return. The copy on your Apple Watch is described under Widgets and Apple Watch.

Deleting Tendi from your iPhone removes its data from the iPhone, apart from a few keychain records with no journal content and the "What's coming" dismissals in iCloud. It does not delete data synced to iCloud, the copies in your device backups or Apple Health, or the copy on an Apple Watch that still has Tendi. Synced data may remain or return when sync resumes. Use Delete All Data and Apple's iCloud data controls as applicable to manage those copies.

You can ask us to delete launch-list or support-email records by contacting us. The limits on deleting analytics held by TelemetryDeck are explained above.

Legal bases for processing

Tendi processes your journal, Apple Health data and on-device AI inputs on your devices, and iCloud sync uses your own Apple account. We do not receive that data. For the personal data that we or our providers do receive, the legal bases under the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) are:

  • Optional app analytics: your consent (GDPR Article 6(1)(a)). You can withdraw it at any time in Settings. Withdrawing does not affect analytics sent before.
  • Launch-updates emails: your consent, given when you sign up. You can unsubscribe from any email.
  • Support emails and bug reports: our legitimate interest in answering you and fixing the problems you report (Article 6(1)(f)).
  • Hosting and securing this website: our legitimate interest in delivering the site and preventing abuse (Article 6(1)(f)).
  • The public feedback site: our legitimate interest in collecting product feedback (Article 6(1)(f)). Posting and signing in are your choice.

Your rights

Under the GDPR, you can ask us for a copy of the personal data we hold about you, and ask us to correct it, delete it, restrict how we use it, or give it to you in a portable format. You can object to uses based on our legitimate interests, and you can withdraw consent at any time. To make a request, write to hello [at] twodesks [dot] app.

Your journal does not reach us unless you send it to us, so you can export, edit or delete it yourself in the app. We may not be able to find your analytics events, because they are linked only to a hashed random identifier.

You can also complain to a data protection authority. In Greece, this is the Hellenic Data Protection Authority (Αρχή Προστασίας Δεδομένων Προσωπικού Χαρακτήρα) at www.dpa.gr. You can also complain to the authority in the EU country where you live or work.
